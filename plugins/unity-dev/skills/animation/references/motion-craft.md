# Motion craft

This reference can be wrong or out of date: verify what you rely on, as the [standards](../../standards/SKILL.md#verify-before-relying-on-them) require.

For motion keyed or generated by code: when no library clip fits, for animals, for cartoon proportions, or when the brief forbids third-party content. It can look natural, and it looks robotic by default: a sine wave on each joint is the first thing code writes. These numbers and rules are what a walk is made of. Cartoon timing may push them far on purpose, but nothing goes missing.

## Two legs

Measured on adult humans. Sources are at the end; marked items are weaker.

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Stance, swing | About 60% and 40% of the cycle |
| Double support | About 10% at each end of stance, 20% in all |
| Rise and fall of the body | About 5 cm, lowest with both feet down, highest over the planted leg |
| Side to side | About 6 cm, leaning over the planted foot |
| Pelvis rotation | About 4–5° each way (weak source) |
| Knee | About 15° as weight lands, about 60° in swing (weak source) |
| Cadence | About 100–115 steps a minute |
| Step length | About 0.4–0.45 of height (weak source) |
| Arms | Against the legs: the right arm comes forward with the left leg. No reliable amplitude found; the thorax turns against the pelvis |

## Speed from the body

Speed that doesn't fit the legs is what looks wrong first, even with planted feet.

- **Froude number:** `Fr = v² / (g × L)`, with L the leg length. Bodies of different sizes move alike at the same Froude number. Humans change from walking to running at about 0.5, whatever their leg length.
- **A comfortable walk** is about Fr 0.2, as an inference from 1.4 m/s on 0.9 m legs: `v = sqrt(0.2 × 9.81 × L)`. A chibi with 0.3 m legs walks at about 0.75 m/s, and can't walk faster than about 1.2 m/s. `inspect_animation.py` reports `froude` and `natural_walk_speed_mps`.
- **Short legs step faster,** not only travel slower: step frequency goes with `sqrt(g / L)`.
- **Stride:** Alexander's `stride / hip height = 2.3 × (v² / (g × hip height))^0.3`, which gives the stride to key for a speed. It was fitted to estimate speeds from footprints: a starting point, not a law.
- **Heavy animals** walk at lower Froude numbers: elephants prefer about 0.09.
- **Game speed comes last.** If the game wants a character faster than its legs allow, change the proportions, use a run, or scale the world: don't play a walk at double speed. `inspect_animation.py` with `GAME_SPEED` reports the playback rate; no source gives a believable range, and ±25% is a working default to check by eye.

## Four legs

Described by duty factor, the share of the cycle a foot is down, and when each foot lands.

| Gait | Footfalls | Duty factor |
|---|---|---|
| Walk | Four beats in lateral sequence: hind left, fore left, hind right, fore right, a quarter of the cycle apart | Above 0.5; about 0.55–0.75 in horses |
| Trot | Two beats: diagonal pairs land together | Near or below 0.5 |
| Pace | Two beats: same-side pairs land together | Near or below 0.5 |
| Gallop, bound | The hind pair, then the fore pair, nearly together or close in sequence | Well below 0.5 |

Species differ (sources are second-hand; look at video of the real animal before keying):

- **Horses** go from walk to trot at about 2 m/s.
- **Elephants** always keep a foot on the ground and stay in lateral sequence at all speeds; forelegs stay down longer than hind legs.
- **Penguins** waddle: short steps with the body rocking side to side like a pendulum.
- **Kangaroos** move slowly on five limbs, with the tail on the ground, and hop when fast, with crouched hind legs.
- **Rabbits** hop or bound; no measured numbers found.

`inspect_animation.py` with `FEET` and `GAIT` reads the footfall order and duty factor from the clip.

## Why code looks robotic, and the fix

From animation craft (Disney's principles, Richard Williams' *The Animator's Survival Kit*): no numbers, but each has a test in the review.

| Robotic | Natural | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Every joint turns at constant speed | Slow in and slow out: ease toward each extreme | `linear_motion` |
| Everything peaks on the same frame | Overlap: the hips lead, the spine follows, then the shoulders, head and hands, each a few frames later | `everything_moves_together` |
| The body glides level | It rises and falls, leans, and turns against its own hips | `no_pelvis_motion`, `trunk_still` |
| Straight lines between poses | Arcs: hands, feet and the head trace curves | The filmstrip |
| Stiff limbs swinging from the root | Successive breaking of joints: the thigh leads and the knee trails, the upper arm leads and the forearm drags | `stiff_knees`, `arms_hang_still` |
| Perfect symmetry | Small differences between the sides, and a loop long enough not to read as a metronome | The filmstrip |
| Dead tails, ears, hair, bags | Follow-through: they trail the body and settle after it stops | `secondary_still` |
| Everything keeps moving, or freezes | Moving holds: a pose stays with tiny motion | `frozen` |
| Poses without weight | Weight over the standing foot, and anticipation before a move: a small move the other way first | The filmstrip |

For cartoons, add squash and stretch, keeping the volume constant; on a skeleton it comes from scaling bones along their length, which Humanoid doesn't keep, so it needs a Generic rig or the shader.

## How to key it

1. **Choose the speed from the body,** then the stride and the cycle's length.
2. **Plant the feet first,** in world or body space, with IK: contacts on whole frames, foot flat through stance, heel then toe. Everything else hangs from them.
3. **Then the hips:** rise, fall, lean, turn, in the phases above.
4. **Then outward, each joint later than its parent:** spine, chest (turning against the hips), head (staying steady on the horizon), upper arms, forearms, hands, tail, ears.
5. **Check, then look:** `inspect_animation.py`, then a filmstrip with a fixed camera. Compare with video of the real thing, frame by frame, when you can find one.

A clip that runs on a GPU bone texture or a custom format still gets checked: key or rebuild it on a Blender armature and run the same script, or the numbers stay unproven.
